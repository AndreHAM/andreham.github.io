I study how energy can be stored, protected, and controlled in open quantum systems. My research connects quantum thermodynamics and quantum information to understand the energetic behaviour of interacting systems and develop strategies for quantum batteries and thermal devices.

I am based at the International Centre for Theory of Quantum Technologies, University of Gdańsk, where I lead the NCN-funded SONATINA project “Modular quantum thermal devices: integrating thermal functionalities” (2025–2028). The project investigates how heat manipulation and energy storage can be combined within integrated quantum devices, with the goal of developing designs suited to superconducting circuits.

Research

Quantum energy storage, heat-flow control and local energetics are the main threads of my research. We proposed a Schmidt-decomposition approach that preserves energy additivity in interacting autonomous systems, including strong coupling.

Recent contributions

  • Quantum batteries: Chiral squeezing (2026), charge-preserving operations (2026), and ergotropy protection (2025).
  • Heat transport: Detuning and thermal-device functionality (2024).
  • Foundations: Schmidt decomposition and local energetics (2022).

Collaborate

I welcome collaborations with theorists and experimentalists working on quantum batteries, heat transport, open-system dynamics and superconducting quantum circuits.

Potential starting points include developing experimentally testable battery-protection protocols, modelling devices that combine thermal functions, and investigating energetic constraints in interacting quantum systems. I am particularly interested in projects where analytical modelling and numerical analysis can help connect a theoretical proposal to an experimental platform.

To discuss a project or research question, email andrehamalavazi at gmail.com.

Background

I completed my PhD in quantum thermodynamics at the University of São Paulo in 2022 under Frederico Brito (academic tree), followed by postdoctoral research at ICTQT in Michał Horodecki’s New Quantum Resources and Thermodynamics Group. My earlier research in MEMS and neuroengineering, together with collaborative work in stochastic thermodynamics, informs my interest in connecting physical models with practical systems.
